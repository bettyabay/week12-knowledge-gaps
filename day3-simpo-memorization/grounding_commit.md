## What Today's Research Revealed

The datasheet flagged the programmatic skew as a limitation but did not explain *why* it is a risk. Day 3's research provides the precise mechanism: programmatic pairs are generated by parameter sweeps across slot-filled templates, which creates vocabulary correlations between chosen and rejected labels. A judge trained on these pairs without sufficient lexical variety may learn surface phrasing — associating specific words with SUPPRESS or SEND — rather than the underlying decision rule.

This maps directly onto the tenacious-bench dataset: 36% of pairs are programmatic, which is enough to dominate vocabulary statistics in the training distribution. The 44% trace-derived and 20% hand-authored pairs provide genuine lexical diversity and partially counteract this risk, but the datasheet gave no explanation of why that diversity matters. The limitation was identified but underspecified.

Crucially, this is different from Samuel's setup: his adapter used 200 template-only pairs with no trace-derived or hand-authored counterpart. The tenacious-bench dataset has structural protection against vocabulary memorization — but that protection was invisible in the datasheet because the mechanism was not articulated.

## The Edit

Extended limitation #1 to explain the vocabulary memorization mechanism and add a concrete diagnostic recommendation — test on paraphrased variants before trusting eval accuracy on the programmatic-heavy dev split.
